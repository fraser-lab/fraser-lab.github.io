description: "James was an undergraduate at McGill University, where he worked in the lab of [Dr. Francois Fagotto](https://www.crbm.cnrs.fr/francois-fagotto/?lang=en) on [Xenopus developmental biology](/publications#20974811).
During the summers, he returned to his hometown of Toronto and worked in [Dr. Alan Davidson's lab](https://individual.utoronto.ca/Davidsonlab/) on TetR repressor biophysics and [bacteriophage genomics](/publications#16631788).


He moved to California in 2005 to do his Ph.D. in [Molecular and Cellular Biology at UC Berkeley](https://mcb.berkeley.edu).
There, he worked with [Dr. Tom Alber](https://fraserlab.com/2014/04/04/Tom-Alber/) creating  biophysical methods to characterize [protein side chain flexibility](/publications#20499387) in [high resolution](/publications#17573816) X-ray [electron density maps](/publications#24363322).
They applied these techniques to study connections between [conformational dynamics and enzymatic catalysis](/publications#19956261), showing that [room temperature](/publications#21918110), but not standard cryogenic, X-ray data collection could reveal the structural basis for critical functional motions.


Near the end of his Ph.D., he was an [EMBO Short-Term Fellow](https://www.embo.org/funding/fellowships-grants-and-career-support/scientific-exchange-grants/) in [Dr. Dan Tawfik's lab](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/Biomolecular_Sciences/obituary/prof-dan-tawfik) at the Weizmann Institute of Science.
Concurrently, he authored the problems and solutions manual for the physical chemistry textbook [The Molecules of Life](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Molecules_of_Life.html?id=7pgAEAAAQBAJ) by Kuriyan, Konforti, and Wemmer.


In January 2011, James started his [independent career](https://fellows.ucsf.edu/) as a [QB3 at UCSF Fellow](https://fellows.ucsf.edu/former-fellows) affiliated with the [Department of Cellular and Molecular Pharmacology](https://cmp.ucsf.edu/).
In January 2013, he was appointed as an Assistant Professor in the [Department of Bioengineering and Therapeutic Sciences](https://bts.ucsf.edu) and the California Institute for Quantitative Biosciences ([QB3](https://qb3.org)) with promotion to Associate Professor in 2016, and Full Professor in 2020. From 2022-2023, he was Vice Dean of Research for the [UCSF School of Pharmacy](https://pharmacy.ucsf.edu/). Since August 2023, he has been the Ernest L. Prien Professor and Chair of the [Department of Bioengineering and Therapeutic Sciences](https://pharmacy.ucsf.edu/news/2023/08/fraser-named-chair-ucsf-department-bioengineering-therapeutic-sciences).


The lab is also part of the [Macromolecular Structure Group at UCSF](https://msg.ucsf.edu). It maintains a deep connection with Macromolecular Crystallography [Beamline 8.3.1.](https://bl831.als.lbl.gov/~mcfuser/), directed by [Dr. James Holton](https://bl831.als.lbl.gov/~jamesh/), at the [Advanced Light Source](https://als.lbl.gov/). James is also a Faculty Scientist in the [Molecular Biophysics and Integrated Bioimaging Division](https://biosciences.lbl.gov/mbib/) of [Lawrence Berkeley National Lab](https://www.lbl.gov/), and Associate Director of the [Quantitative Biosciences Institute](https://qbi.ucsf.edu/). He is a member of the [Helen Diller Family Comprehensive Cancer Center](https://cancer.ucsf.edu/people/membership). He is the co-director of the Bakar Computational Biomedicine Initiative, a joint venture between UCSF and UC Berkeley. 


The lab participates in multiple collaborative initiatives. James is a Governing Board Member of the [OpenADMET](https://openadmet.org/) project, which  accelerates drug discovery by focusing on the chemical space recognized by the \"anti-targets\" that most projects seek to avoid. The lab is also part of the [diffUSE project](https://diffuse.science/), an open science initiative of [Radial](https://radial.org/), a division of [the Astera Institute](https://astera.org/), that is developing the experimental tooling, computational methods, and data systems needed to make molecular motion visible, measurable and usable.


In addition to all the exciting developments in the lab, James has long standing interests in [teaching](/courses), [baseball statistics](https://www.ibiology.org/professional-development/baseball-meets-biology-baseball-statistics-biology/), and [juggling](https://pharmacy.ucsf.edu/news/2020/10/labs-juggle-community-distance-during-covid-19). For many years, James was on the board of ASAPbio, a non-profit organization dedicated to accelerating the pace of scientific discovery by making research outputs more accessible and reusable. The lab is committed to [publishing our code](https://github.com/fraser-lab), disseminating our datasets, posting manuscripts on preprint servers, and [participating in open peer review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYG5wTGh6aw&list=PLezPfyQ6lSolc6sOZ-an18oG3mZojuwb6&t=1280s). 


He is the co-creator of [UCSF Talks](https://ucsftalks.com), an aggregator of all the discovery science seminars at UCSF, and records the [Tortured Proteins Department Podcast](https://open.spotify.com/show/10PHVouHr7pY8Q1p4QJ2EQ) with [Prof. Stephanie Wankowicz](https://wankowiczlab.com/).


His full [conflicts of interest](/coi) are listed on a separate page."
